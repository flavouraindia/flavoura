How to Make Restaurant-Style Gravy at Home in 15 Minutes

By Flavoura5 October 20263 min read

Restaurant gravy gets its taste from slow-cooked onions, fresh pastes and freshly ground spices. At home, that prep can take over an hour. An instant premix lets you skip all of it.

Why restaurant gravy tastes different

A restaurant kitchen starts hours before you order. Onions are cooked down slowly, ginger and garlic are ground fresh, whole spices are roasted and ground, and nuts or seeds are blended in to give the gravy body. That is where the rich colour, the silky texture and the layered flavour come from. Most quick home recipes skip those steps, which is why home gravy often tastes thinner.

What is already in the premix

Flavoura gravy premixes bring that base together in one pouch. The Red, White and Golden Gravy premixes all contain cashew, melon seeds and white sesame for creaminess, along with milk powder, onion, garlic and ginger powders and a blend of ground spices. You do not chop, peel or grind anything. Salt is already in the pack, so taste the finished gravy before adding more. Check the ingredients on each product card if you want the full list.

Pick your gravy

  • Red Gravy: a classic North Indian gravy base that works with paneer, vegetables or chicken.
  • White Gravy: a rich, creamy Mughlai-style gravy and the mildest of the range.
  • Golden Gravy: a rich cashew and sesame base that suits shahi paneer, vegetables or chicken.

What you need

  • 1 Flavoura gravy premix
  • Paneer, vegetables or your favourite protein
  • Curd or tomato puree
  • A little oil or butter

Steps

  1. Heat oil or butter in a pan.
  2. Add the premix with curd or tomato puree and stir well.
  3. Add your main ingredient and cook until done.
  4. Adjust the thickness with water and serve hot.

Amounts for each gravy

These are the quantities for a full-size pack and 500 g of your main ingredient.

  • Red Gravy (paneer or veg): cook 400 g tomato puree in oil, then add 80 g premix mixed to a paste with water and 500 g paneer or vegetables.
  • Red Gravy (chicken): marinate 500 g chicken with 80 g premix and curd or water, then add it to the cooked tomato puree.
  • White Gravy (paneer): mix 60 g premix with milk to a paste, cook it in butter or oil until thick, then add 500 g paneer.
  • White Gravy (chicken): marinate 500 g chicken with 60 g premix and 200 g curd, then cook in butter or oil.
  • Golden Gravy: cook 400 g tomato puree with the full 80 g premix and 500 g protein or vegetables in oil until thick.

Tips for a better gravy

  • Stir well when the premix goes in so it blends smoothly with no lumps.
  • Add water a little at a time until the gravy is as thick or thin as you like.
  • Finish with a small knob of butter if you want a richer, glossier gravy.
  • Serve with roti, naan, paratha or steamed rice.
Tip: use less liquid for a dry tikka-style dish and more for a rich gravy.
